Cannon Hop Privacy Policy

Effective: 27 November 2026

Cannon Hop is a game made by Twelve Doors Interactive ("we"). This policy explains what happens to information when you play.

The short version: there are no accounts and we don't ask who you are. Act 1 is free; one purchase through Google Play or the App Store opens the full game. The only ad is one you choose to watch to keep an endless run going, shown by Google AdMob. If you pick a name for the leaderboards, that name, your best scores and the motto you choose are shown to other players. If you join a crew, the friends in it see the same. If you say yes to game statistics, the game sends a few anonymous statistics so we can make it better.

1. Information that stays on your phone

The game saves on your device only: your best score, your story progress (chapters finished, stars, posters found, best chapter scores, last checkpoint), how your stunt performer looks, and your tickets, daily missions, streak and the looks you own, your settings (sound, reminders, your game statistics choice), the players you blocked, and whether you own the full game. None of this is sent to us. Uninstalling the game, or clearing its data in your phone's settings, deletes it.

2. Leaderboards and crews (Unity Gaming Services)

The title screen has a TOP 20 board of the best single endless runs and a TODAY board for the Daily Challenge. To appear on them you pick a name (3 to 10 letters or numbers; rude words are refused). The game then uses Unity Gaming Services (Unity Technologies):

  • Anonymous sign-in: Unity creates a random player ID so the board can keep one row per player. On a phone the same ID is kept between launches. Uninstalling the game or clearing its data makes a new one. It is not linked to your name, email or store account.
  • What is stored and shown: your chosen name, your best endless score, your best Daily Challenge score for the day and your motto are stored on Unity's servers and shown to other players on the boards. A motto is one of a fixed list of short lines; you can't type your own. Unity sees your phone's IP address when the game reads or updates the board.
  • Not picking a name: without a name, your score is never sent. Anyone can still look at the board.
  • Crews: you can start a crew, which gives you a 6-letter code, or join one with a code a friend gives you (up to 20 players). The game stores your crew code, your name and your motto as public player data in Unity Cloud Save, so the players in your crew can see your name, motto and scores on the MY CREW board. There is no chat and no way to search for players: only someone who has the code can see a crew. Leaving the crew deletes this data from Unity's servers.
  • Reporting a player: tap another player on a board, then Report, and pick a reason from a short list. The game opens our feedback form (Google Forms) with the board, that player's name, motto, random player ID and the reason already filled in. Nothing is sent until you tap Submit on the form. We use reports only to remove names or scores that break the rules.
  • Blocking a player: tap another player on a board, then Block, to hide them from your boards and your crew list. The list of players you blocked is kept only on your phone, and nobody is told. You can unblock them in Settings > Blocked players.
  • Removing your entry: email us with your name and we'll take it off the boards.

Unity's privacy policy for game players.

3. Game statistics (Unity Analytics), only if you say yes

The first time you open the game it asks whether it may share anonymous game statistics. If you tap Sure!, it sends these to Unity Analytics (Unity Technologies):

  • that the game was opened for the first time, and your first endless run and first story chapter;
  • for endless runs: the score, how far you got, how the run ended, your best score, the course direction, and how long it lasted;
  • for story chapters: when a chapter starts, falls (where, and from which checkpoint), finishing (stars, falls, posters, score, time) or leaving a chapter early;
  • how long each comic page stayed open, and which screen you were on when you left the game;
  • opening the full-game card (and from where), buying the full game, continuing an endless run after a fall, and whether a continue ad was watched to the end. Unity's purchase tools may also record the purchase itself (product and price), never your payment details.

With these, Unity receives a random installation ID, your device model and operating system version, the game version, and your approximate country (worked out from your IP address). None of it is linked to your name, email or store account, and we never see who you are.

  • Saying no: tap No thanks and the game sends no statistics at all.
  • Changing your mind: tap Settings > Game stats on the title screen at any time. Turning statistics off stops them and asks Unity to delete what it has for your phone. Uninstalling the game or clearing its data also starts a new random ID.

Unity's privacy policy for game players.

4. Advertising and purchases

Ads (Google AdMob). Cannon Hop shows one kind of ad: after a fall in an endless run, you can choose to watch a short video ad to keep the run going. There are no ads in story mode or the Daily Challenge, no banners, and no ads that interrupt play. To show and measure ads, Google may collect your device's advertising ID, your IP address (and the approximate location it suggests), device and app information, and how you interact with the ad. How Google uses information from apps that use its services.

  • Your choice: in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland, and where other laws require it, Google asks for your consent before ads are personalised. You can change your answer at any time from Settings > Ad privacy choices (the button appears where the law requires it). If you don't consent, you may still be offered ads, but they won't be personalised.
  • Advertising ID: you can reset or delete it in your phone's settings (on Android: Settings > Privacy > Ads).

We do not sell your personal information. For California residents: because ads may be personalised, Google's use of data can count as "sharing" for cross-context behavioural advertising under California law. You can opt out from Settings > Ad privacy choices or by deleting your advertising ID.

The full-game purchase. Act 1 is free. One purchase opens Acts 2 to 5, future chapters and a free continue in endless runs. Payment is handled entirely by Google Play or the App Store under their own terms; we never see your card or account details. The game keeps a note on your phone that you own the full game, and asks the store again when you reinstall or tap Restore purchase.

5. Children

Cannon Hop is meant for players 13 and older and is not directed at children under 13. We don't knowingly collect information from children under 13. If you believe a child has put a name on the leaderboard or shared game statistics, contact us and we'll help remove it.

6. Your rights

Because we don't hold any information about you, there is usually nothing for us to show, correct or delete. Statistics held by Unity are covered by its policy, and turning statistics off in the game asks Unity to delete them. We are also happy to help with any request. If you live in the EEA or UK, you also have the right to complain to your local data protection authority.

Legal bases (EEA/UK): showing your name, motto and best scores on the leaderboards and to your crew is necessary to provide the features you chose by picking a name or joining a crew; game statistics are sent only with your consent; personalised advertising, where Google's consent form applies, is based on your consent; non-personalised ads rely on our legitimate interest in funding the free part of the game; the purchase is handled to fulfil your order.

7. Security

Leaderboard entries, crew data and statistics are sent encrypted to Unity's servers, and ad requests encrypted to Google. Information on your phone is protected by your phone's own security.

8. Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update this page and the date at the top. Important changes will also be mentioned in the game's update notes.
