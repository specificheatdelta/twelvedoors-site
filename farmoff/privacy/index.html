FarmOff! Privacy Policy

Effective: 9 November 2026

FarmOff! is a game made by Twelve Doors Interactive ("we"). This policy explains what happens to information when you play.

The short version: we don't have accounts, we don't ask who you are, and we don't run any servers that store information about you. The services the game uses to work, listed below, do receive some technical information, as any app that goes online does.

1. Information that stays on your phone

The game saves your progress on your device only: Golden Seeds, unlocked farmers and crops, your chosen settings (difficulty, season length, farmer, text size), whether you've seen the intro, and how many online matches you've played today. None of this is sent to us. Uninstalling the game, or clearing its data in Android settings, deletes it.

2. Online play and the Zen leaderboard (Unity Gaming Services)

When you host or join an online match, or open the Zen leaderboard, the game uses Unity Gaming Services (Unity Technologies):

  • Anonymous sign-in: Unity creates a random player ID so its servers can tell players apart. On a phone the same ID is kept between launches, so your place on the Zen leaderboard stays yours. Uninstalling the game or clearing its data makes a new one. It is not linked to your name, email or Google account.
  • Relay: the game's moves pass through Unity's servers between the two phones, so Unity sees your phone's IP address while you play. The match itself (farm positions, crops, money) is only passed along, not kept.
  • Zen leaderboard: before your first Zen farm the game asks you to pick a farm name (3 to 10 letters or digits; rude words are refused). Your farm name and your lifetime Zen earnings are stored on Unity's servers and shown to other players on the leaderboard. Other players can report a name. To have your entry removed, email us with your farm name.
  • Unity's services may also record basic technical diagnostics, for example errors in its own software.

Unity's privacy policy: unity.com/legal/privacy-policy

Online play check. Before an online match starts, the game downloads a small settings file from this website that says whether online play is open. The website is hosted by GitHub Pages, which sees your IP address for that request, as with any web page. Nothing about you is sent. GitHub's privacy statement.

Playing solo, or over local Wi-Fi, uses none of these services, apart from the game statistics below.

3. Game statistics (Unity Analytics)

To learn where new players get stuck, the game sends a few anonymous statistics to Unity Analytics (Unity Technologies): that the game was opened, whether the first lesson was started, finished or skipped, whether the first season was finished, and the first visit to Zen mode. For solo seasons it also sends when a season starts, ends or is quit, with the season's settings (difficulty, length, its seasonal twist, whether the farm kitchen was open, and your text size setting) and, at the end, the result (medal, haypence earned, crops lost and dishes sold). With these, Unity receives a random installation ID, your device model and Android version, the game version, and your approximate country (worked out from your IP address). None of it is linked to your name, email or Google account, and we never see who you are.

  • When it starts: statistics are only sent after the game's privacy step. Where Google's consent form applies (the European Economic Area, the UK, Switzerland and other places where the law requires it), they follow your answer to that form.
  • Turning it off: tap Privacy → Stop game statistics on the game's start screen. The game stops sending statistics and asks Unity to delete what it has for your phone. You can turn them back on from the same button. Uninstalling the game or clearing its data also starts a new random ID.

Unity's privacy policy for game players.

4. Advertising

The free version of FarmOff! shows ads from Google AdMob. To show and measure ads, Google may collect your device's advertising ID, your IP address (and the approximate location it suggests), device and app information, and how you interact with ads. How Google uses information from apps that use its services.

  • Your choice: in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland, and where other laws require it, Google asks for your consent before showing personalised ads. You can change your answer at any time from Privacy → Ad choices on the game's start screen. If you don't consent, you may still see ads, but they won't be personalised.
  • Advertising ID: you can reset or delete it in Android settings (Settings → Privacy → Ads).

We do not sell your personal information. For California residents: because ads may be personalised, Google's use of data can count as "sharing" for cross-context behavioural advertising under California law. You can opt out from Privacy → Ad choices or by deleting your advertising ID.

5. Children

FarmOff! is meant for players 13 and older and is not directed at children under 13. We don't knowingly collect information from children under 13. If you believe a child has used online play, put a name on the leaderboard or seen personalised ads, contact us and we'll help.

6. Your rights

Because we don't hold any information about you, there is usually nothing for us to show, correct or delete. Data held by Unity, GitHub or Google is covered by their policies, and you can contact them directly. We are also happy to help with any request. If you live in the EEA or UK, you also have the right to complain to your local data protection authority.

Legal bases (EEA/UK): relaying online matches and showing your Zen leaderboard entry are necessary to provide the features you chose; personalised advertising, and game statistics where the consent form applies, are based on your consent; non-personalised ads, basic diagnostics and game statistics elsewhere rely on our legitimate interest in running, improving and funding a free game.

7. Security

The game's online traffic is encrypted by Unity's services. Information on your phone is protected by your phone's own security.

8. Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update this page and the date at the top. Important changes will also be mentioned in the game's update notes.
